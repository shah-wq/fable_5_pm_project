Chapter 13

Sales and signing

In residential solar the first minutes after an enquiry decide a large share of the outcome, and most of the sales team's administration is copying: from a web form into a contact, from a bill into a deal, from a proposal into a contract.

RecommendationHow it works in SolarFlowPeople after
S1Web and ad leads straight inHighSWebsite forms and ad platforms (Meta and Google lead forms) post to a signed endpoint that calls the existing create_contact, with UTM source, medium and campaign. The duplicate check across every email and phone runs as it does today.No re-typing. Suspected duplicates go to exceptions.
S2Speed-to-leadHighSOn contact.created, within 60 seconds, an SMS and email go out (B5) with the rep's name and a booking link. If nothing is booked in 15 minutes, a call task goes to the owner. All inside the contact's consent and quiet hours.The rep calls only the leads who have not booked themselves.
S3RoutingMediumSThe owner is chosen by rules: ZIP-code territory, language, dealer, rep capacity (open deals) and availability, round-robin within a team. The owner field already exists; today it defaults to the creator.No morning lead-assignment meeting.
S4Self-booking, reminders, no-show recoveryHighMA booking page over the reps' calendars (e.g. Google Calendar or Cal.com). Booking moves the contact to Appointment scheduled. Reminders go 24 hours and 2 hours before, each with a reschedule link, which moves the contact to Rescheduled. A missed appointment moves it to No-show and starts a rebooking sequence. The contact stages board already has these columns.No phone tag. The board moves by itself.
S5Utility-bill readerHighSThe bill photos already uploaded on the deal (electricity_bill_front/back) are read by a vision model into the deal's typed fields: utility, rate plan, monthly and annual kWh, average bill. Structured output is validated against the schema.The rep confirms low-confidence reads only.
S6Instant preliminary design and priceHighLAddress plus usage go to a roof and sunlight service (e.g. Google Solar API, or a design tool's API such as Aurora or OpenSolar), which returns the usable roof, module count, system size and estimated production. The price book and adder rules (both tables exist) produce the price, and the result becomes a draft proposal version on the deal.The rep reviews and presents it; the designer finalises after signing.
S7Financing status syncHighMThe lender application link is sent from the deal. The lender's webhook or API (e.g. GoodLeap, Mosaic, Sunlight, LightReach) writes the decision, amount and terms to the deal, and an approval moves the contact to Financing approved. Stipulations become asks to the homeowner.Nobody checks lender portals.
S8Contract out automaticallyHighSBuilt on the new e-signature: when a deal reaches Financing approved (or cash with a deposit) and its proposal is accepted, the PandaDoc contract is sent with the proposal's system. Reminders go out on days 1, 3 and 5 if it is unsigned, then a task for the rep. Signing creates the project as it does today.The rep no longer prepares or chases contracts.
S9Call and meeting notesMediumMRecorded calls, or the rep's voice memo after an appointment, are transcribed and summarised into the activity log: objections, next step, decision makers, roof or shading notes, competitor. Any agreed follow-up date becomes the deal's next action.Reps stop writing notes; the next action is never blank.

Chapter 15

Permits and interconnection

The longest wait in most projects, and the one people check most often. The work is rarely hard. It is repetitive: filling the same forms, then checking portals until something changes.

RecommendationHow it works in SolarFlowPeople after
P1Jurisdiction knowledge baseHighMPer jurisdiction and utility (both tables exist): the required documents, fees, submission method, typical turnaround (measured from our own permit history) and common rejection reasons. It is kept current by the people who hit a change, and read by P2, SD5 and Ask SolarFlow.The knowledge stops living in one person's head.
P2Permit packet assemblyHighMFrom P1's list, the system gathers the plan set, stamped plans, spec sheets and site plan, fills the jurisdiction's application form from project data, and produces the packet ready to submit, or submits it where the portal has an API.The permit coordinator checks and submits in minutes.
P3Instant permits where availableHighMFor jurisdictions on SolarAPP+ (automated permitting for standard residential systems), qualifying projects go through it and get the permit the same day.Zero touches for qualifying systems.
P4Status watcherHighMEach open permit, ICA and HOA track is watched: portal APIs where they exist, a monitored inbox (the permit office's and utility's emails, read by D1 and D2), and a scheduled check on quiet portals. Changes write permit_events and the stage-form status. A track silent for longer than P1's typical turnaround gets a follow-up email and a task.Nobody checks portals. The PM sees changes and overdue items.
P5HOA packet and letterMediumSWhen the HOA track is required, a letter and packet are generated (site plan, array render, spec sheets) and sent to the homeowner to forward, or to the HOA directly. The approval comes back through D5 and D2.No hand-built HOA packets.
P6Rejection triageMediumSA rejection or correction letter is read and turned into a list of changes, assigned to design or to the permit coordinator, with the revision notes filled in on the permit track. Each rejection reason is added to P1 so the next packet avoids it.Corrections start the same day the letter arrives.
