Chapter 16 · Roadmap

Future AI integrations

Where judgement, reading or language is involved and rules alone cannot finish the job. For each capability: what the AI does, where its answer goes, how far it can act alone, and where a person stays in the loop.

Recommended model approach

Use one server-side integration with the Anthropic Claude API, as Ask SolarFlow already does: Claude Opus 5 for reasoning, drafting and reading documents; a cheaper model (Claude Sonnet 5 or Claude Haiku 4.5) only for high-volume classification and routing, once measured against the same examples; and vision input for photos and scanned documents. Pair it with solar data services: Google Solar API or aerial imagery providers (e.g. EagleView, Nearmap) for roof geometry and sunlight, and NREL PVWatts for production estimates. Every AI result is written through the same API and database rules as a person's.

Guardrails for every AI feature

Confidence and the exceptions queue

Every AI action carries a confidence. Below the threshold it goes to exceptions for a person; above it, it acts. Thresholds are per capability and tuned from real outcomes.

People for money, law and safety

Prices over a limit, contract terms, engineering sign-off, electrical safety and anything a regulator requires a licensed person for always stay with a person.

Consent and disclosure

AI texts and calls only with prior express written consent, within quiet hours, honouring do-not-call; the AI identifies itself. Consent basis is stored (as M26 already does).

Measured, logged, reversible

Every AI action is in the audit log under an AI actor. Each capability has a test set it must pass before release and a dashboard of accuracy and cost.

Chapter 17 · Roadmap

Autonomy roadmap

A realistic path from today to a pipeline where people handle only exceptions and the work that law and physics reserve for them.

Levels of autonomy

LevelNameMeaning
L0ManualA person does the work; the system records it. (Most stage data entry today.)
L1AssistedThe system checks and blocks mistakes. (Stage validation, duplicate guard, hold: today.)
L2DraftedAI prepares the work; a person approves with one click.
L3SupervisedAI or rules act on their own; people review exceptions and samples.
L4AutonomousThe step runs end to end; people see results and outliers only.

Phases

PhaseWhenScope
0 · Switch onWeeks 0–2Schedule the reminders job; fix the database hosting (integration, default branch, backups, rotated password); confirm email and push configuration; add CI for the test suites; fit the Pipeline board to the screen.
1 · ConnectMonths 1–2SMS provider; self-booking calendar; speed-to-lead sequence; e-signature (built — switch on); lender status sync; monitoring platform API; report schedule runner; staff two-factor sign-in.
2 · AI drafts (L2)Months 2–4Utility-bill reader; document classification; conversation summaries; homeowner copilot drafting replies; survey and install photo QA flags; preliminary design drafts.
3 · AI acts (L3)Months 4–8Copilot answers routine status questions itself; permit/interconnection monitoring writes statuses; survey auto-scheduling; evidence-based auto-advance; adders and quotes below a price limit; cancellation-risk outreach.
4 · Near zero-touch (L4)Months 8–12AI appointment setter; permit packet submission where portals allow; purchase orders; install scheduling optimisation; forecasting; auto-merge of high-confidence duplicates.

What stays with people

"No human interaction" is achievable for the office work: data entry, chasing, scheduling, status updates, filing and reporting. It is not achievable, and should not be attempted, for the steps that require a licensed or accountable person: the homeowner's signature and financing consent; a licensed engineer's stamp; the physical survey and installation; the jurisdiction's inspection; the utility's permission to operate; and final decisions on pricing exceptions, cancellations and refunds. The target is that people spend their time only on those steps and on the exceptions queue.
