Best practices — solar operations

Practices that consistently separate installers with short cycle times and few cancellations from the rest. Where the platform already supports a practice, it says so; where it could, that is noted.

Selling the right system

  • Size from twelve months of usage, not one bill. Read the full year (the utility-bill reader in chapter 16 makes this effortless) and size for the target offset under the utility's current export rules.
  • Understand the export tariff. Where net metering has been replaced by lower export credits (for example California's net billing tariff), self-consumption matters more and batteries become part of the value, not an add-on.
  • Estimate production honestly. Use a recognised model (PVWatts or your design tool) with real shading, and quote a central (P50) figure. Overpromised savings are the leading source of complaints and cancellations.
  • Check the roof and the panel before the contract. A roof with little life left should be replaced first; a main-panel upgrade discovered at survey is the most common surprise adder. Capture both on the deal before signing.
  • Check the HOA and the utility early. HOA approval and interconnection limits can each stop a job; the permit tracks already hold both, so start them at signing, not after design.
  • Treat incentives as time-sensitive facts. The US residential clean energy credit (IRC §25D) ended for systems paid for after 31 December 2025, while third-party-owned systems (lease/PPA) claim business credits with their own deadlines and conditions. State and utility programmes change often. Verify with a tax professional before quoting savings, and date every proposal.
  • Disclose clearly. Price, financing terms, dealer fees, escalators, production assumptions and the buyer's cancellation right. For sales made in the home, the federal three-day cooling-off rule applies, and many states add more.

Running the delivery pipeline

Survey

  • A fixed photo checklist per survey (every roof plane, attic/rafters, main panel with the cover off, meter, service entrance, planned equipment locations).
  • Measure, do not estimate; record roof age and material.
  • Survey within days of signing: the gap is where buyer's remorse grows.

Design

  • Design against the specific jurisdiction's and utility's rules (setbacks, rapid shutdown, labelling, interconnection method).
  • Run a pre-submission checklist to cut revision cycles; track revisions per project.
  • Get engineering stamps in parallel where possible.

Permits & interconnection

  • Maintain a requirements sheet per jurisdiction and utility.
  • Use instant online permitting (e.g. SolarAPP+) where the jurisdiction has adopted it.
  • Submit interconnection as early as the utility allows, in parallel with the building permit.
  • Measure first-time approval rate and chase anything past its typical duration.

Procurement

  • An approved equipment list with alternates ("okay to install comparable brand" is already asked at signing).
  • Confirm stock before scheduling installs; stage material per job.
  • Watch tariff and domestic-content rules that affect cost and incentives.

Install

  • A pre-install call and a day-of arrival message.
  • Safety first: fall protection and electrical safety procedures, every job.
  • A standard photo set before leaving site (array, attachments, conduit runs, labels, rapid-shutdown devices, grounding, inverter/battery, main panel) — the install-photo QA in chapter 16 checks it.

Inspection, PTO & after

  • Self-inspect from the photo set before booking the inspector; track first-time pass rate.
  • Request PTO immediately after passing; activate monitoring at energization.
  • Check production against the estimate at 7 and 30 days; resolve underperformance before the customer notices.

Keeping the homeowner

  • One named project manager and one channel (the platform's chat) for the life of the job.
  • Tell them before they ask: proactive updates at every stage change, typical durations on screen, and a note when something runs long.
  • Measure every stage (the stage ratings) and act on every low score within a day.
  • Ask for the review and the referral at the moment of delight: power on, and a high final rating.

Best practices — CRM & sales

Speed and persistence

  • Respond in minutes, not hours. Contact rates fall steeply after the first five minutes; an automated instant response plus a quick human call is the single biggest lever on close rate.
  • Use a cadence: calls, texts and emails over one to two weeks, then a longer nurture.
  • Protect the appointment: confirmation at booking, reminders the day before and two hours before, and an easy reschedule link. A rescheduled appointment is far better than a no-show.

Data you can trust

  • One system of record. No side spreadsheets; if it is not in the CRM it did not happen.
  • Minimum fields, enforced; everything else optional. The platform requires a surname and a way to reach the person, and no more, so people do not invent data to get past the form.
  • Stop duplicates at entry (the platform checks every email and phone on file) and merge the rest promptly.
  • Attribution from the first touch: source, campaign and dealer on every contact, so cost per sale by source is measurable.

Pipeline discipline

  • Every stage has exit criteria that the system checks (the deal stages already refuse to advance without them).
  • Every open deal has a next action and a date (enforced), and a weekly pipeline review walks through anything overdue.
  • A reason for every loss, from a fixed list (enforced), reviewed monthly; lost is reversible, so re-engage at intervals.
  • Visibility by role and territory, with an unassigned pool so nothing waits for a manager to hand it out.

Consent and compliance

  • Calls and texts: automated or AI-generated calls and texts to mobiles need prior express written consent under the US TCPA (the FCC treats AI voices as artificial voices). Honour do-not-call lists, contact only 8am–9pm local, and check stricter state rules.
  • Email and privacy: CAN-SPAM for commercial email; double opt-in with the consent basis stored (M26 shows it); honour deletion requests (the anonymise function exists for this).

Dealers and partners

Agree lead-acceptance rules and response times in both directions; send each dealer a monthly scorecard (leads, appointment rate, close rate, cancellation rate, cycle time, customer rating); and make commission statements automatic and transparent, which prevents more disputes than any policy document.
